How a record flows

Two-step design: hard gates kill disqualifiers before any score exists, so a big number can never rescue an ineligible opportunity.

Hard gatesSeven kill-tests. Any fail → NO-GO (or Human Review where noted). No score is computed for a gated-out record.
Vendor / procurement layerDistrict-tier records and contract instruments only: vendor path + 8 procurement gates. Target-district carve-out routes here instead of the LEA-demote.
Weighted fit score10 dimensions, each 0–5, weights sum to 100. raw = Σ weight × score ÷ 5.
Confidence penalty−6 / −12 / −20 for unverified critical fields. final = max(0, raw − penalty). Unknown eligibility caps at QUALIFY.
RecommendationThresholds map the final score to GO Tier A/B · QUALIFY · WATCH · NO-GO — plus overrides that no score can outvote.

Program readiness & evidence program_readiness_evidence105 = fits existing Relay programs, data, partnerships, outcomes — no new model needed
Effort & deadline runway effort_deadline_runway105 = simple/medium lift with adequate runway

Confidence penalty

Unknowns carry real operational cost (hardened in the 2026-07-07 cross-family review). final = max(0, raw − penalty).

PenaltyCondition
−0Primary source verified; all critical fields known.
−6One critical field uncertain (amount, deadline, match, or geography).
−12Two critical fields uncertain.
−20Three or more uncertain, stale, or conflicting sources.
Eligibility rule: unknown eligibility caps the recommendation at QUALIFY and applies −12, unless the next action is a fast (<1 week) yes/no eligibility check. "Likely eligible by applicant type" is not "eligible" — capture the exact eligibility quote plus the Relay fact needed to prove it.

Recommendation mapping

✕ NO-GO0–39 · do not pursue
○ WATCH40–54 · monitor / cultivate
◆ QUALIFY55–69 · human review
● GO TIER B70–84 · if capacity exists
● GO TIER A85+ · pursue unless overridden
OverridesAny failed hard gate → NO-GO (state which gate) · any critical unknown → cap at QUALIFY until verified · low pursuit-readiness caps the rec regardless of fit. QUALIFY meansSeek clarification, a partner, or an LOI-only path — not a soft yes.

Decision signals — shown separately, never blended into one number

Fit · 0–100The weighted score above — mission, program, financial, and readiness fit.
Eligibility confidence · H/M/LAlways with the exact eligibility quote, the excluded applicant types, and the Relay fact needed to prove eligibility.
Financial reality · net-to-RelayHeadline award vs what reaches Relay: institutional $ vs student stipends/scholarships vs partner subawards vs required pass-through vs indirect recovery. A large scholarship pass-through (Noyce) is not large institutional revenue.
Pursuit readiness · H/M/LCan Relay realistically mount a competitive application in time, with the required assets? Low readiness caps the recommendation regardless of fit.

Relay calibration — favor / demote / adjacent language
Favoreducator prep · teacher residency · licensure & certification pathways · school leadership · educator diversity · high-need partnerships · outcomes evaluation DemoteDistrict-only / LEA-only / K-12-operator-only grant opportunities default to QUALIFY or NO-GO unless a credible partner/subrecipient role exists. The Phase C carve-out exempts target districts (they route through the vendor path); the demote still applies to non-target LEA-only grants. Adjacent languagehuman capital · talent pipeline · workforce development · educator effectiveness · school improvement · leadership development · postsecondary pathways — educator-prep money often hides behind these; the prefilter logs a dropped-title sample every run (state/audit-<date>.json) and is never assumed lossless.
Partnership hypotheses (Stream 2) — a separate lane, never scored

Trigger: a discovered NOFO requires or favors a high-need LEA partner and joins (by geography, name, or NCES ID) to ≥1 target district. The output is a hypothesis, not an opportunity — it never enters the scored table and never gets a GO/QUALIFY.

A hypothesis becomes a lead only when its feasibility fields resolve:

  • required_partner_type — e.g. "high-need LEA," "IHE + LEA consortium"
  • relay_allowed_role — prime | partner | subrecipient | vendor
  • mou_loi_requirement — is a signed MOU/LOI due with the application?
  • match_cost_share — required match and who carries it
  • cultivation_lead_time → cultivate_by = deadline − lead time
  • named_relationship_owner — who at Relay owns the relationship (or "none — cold")

For the ~15 strategic accounts, the district's USASpending grant-posture note travels with the hypothesis (e.g. Chicago holds a direct TQP 84.336 — the strongest co-applicant signal in the set).

Digest output fields
  • Opportunity summary — 1–2 plain-language sentences: what it funds and who runs it; shown as its own row.
  • Effort / complexity — Low (short LOI / simple form) · Medium (narrative + budget) · High (+ partners / evaluation / data) · Very High (+ mandatory match, MOUs, multiple components). Application complexity, not staff-hours.
  • Funding type — flags money Relay can't use as flexible operating support (scholarship pass-throughs, heavy-involvement cooperative agreements).
  • Past winners — recent recipients under the Assistance Listing via USASpending. Broad CFDAs (47.076 = all of NSF EHR) are approximate; specific ones (84.325) are reliable.
  • Recurrence — typical cycle + predicted next deadline, so WATCH items carry a return date.
Pitfall guards — why the two-step design exists
  • Big award, poor fit → mission fit is the top weight and mission boundary is a hard gate.
  • "Education" treated as auto-relevant → a specific Relay-aligned use is required, not broad ed language.
  • Missed cost-share → match is both a hard gate and a scored burden.
  • Applying too close to deadline → feasibility gate + runway dimension.
  • Chasing cold hypercompetitive federal → win probability + funder relationship reduce the score.
  • Optimistic assumptions on unknowns → confidence penalty + Human Review block a false GO.
  • Confusing contract/RFP revenue with grants → the source-type field forces different treatment.
